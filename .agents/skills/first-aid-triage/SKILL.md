# First Aid & Triage

> ⚠️ **CRITICAL DISCLAIMER**: This skill provides general first aid information
> only. In any medical emergency, call 911 (or your local emergency number)
> immediately. This is NOT a substitute for professional medical care.

## Triage Framework: START (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment)

For multi-casualty or unclear severity, use START:

### Step 1: Can they walk?
- **Yes** → "Minor" (green tag) — direct to safe area, assess later
- **No** → Continue to Step 2

### Step 2: Breathing?
- **Breathing** → Go to Step 3
- **Not breathing** → Open airway
  - Still not breathing → "Deceased/Expectant" (black tag)
  - Breathing resumes → "Immediate" (red tag)

### Step 3: Respiratory rate
- **>30 breaths/min** → "Immediate" (red tag)
- **<30 breaths/min** → Go to Step 4

### Step 4: Perfusion (capillary refill)
- **>2 seconds OR no radial pulse** → "Immediate" (red tag)
- **<2 seconds** → Go to Step 5

### Step 5: Mental status
- **Can't follow commands** → "Immediate" (red tag)
- **Can follow commands** → "Delayed" (yellow tag)

### Priority Tags
| Color | Priority | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| 🔴 Red | Immediate | Life-threatening, needs treatment NOW |
| 🟡 Yellow | Delayed | Serious but stable, treat after reds |
| 🟢 Green | Minor | Walking wounded, can wait |
| ⚫ Black | Deceased/Expectant | Unsalvageable with available resources |

## Common Emergency Protocols

### Severe Bleeding
1. **Apply direct pressure** — firm, continuous pressure with clean cloth/gauze
2. **Elevate** the wound above heart level if possible
3. **Pressure point** — if direct pressure fails, press artery against bone
4. **Tourniquet** — last resort for limb bleeding, 2-3 inches above wound
   - Note time applied
   - Do NOT remove once applied (medical professionals only)
5. **Call 911** if: bleeding doesn't stop in 10 minutes, spurting blood, large wound

### Choking (Adult)
1. Ask "Are you choking?" — if they can speak/cough, encourage coughing
2. **If silent, can't breathe, skin turning blue**:
   - Stand behind them
   - Wrap arms around waist
   - Make fist, thumb side against abdomen, just above navel
   - Grasp fist with other hand
   - Quick inward and upward thrusts
   - Repeat until object expelled or person becomes unconscious
3. **If unconscious**: Call 911, begin CPR

### CPR (Adult — Hands-Only)
1. **Check responsiveness** — shake and shout
2. **Call 911** — or have someone else call
3. **Position**: Person on back on firm surface
4. **Hand placement**: Heel of one hand on center of chest, other hand on top
5. **Compress**: Push hard and fast — 100-120 compressions/min, 2 inches deep
6. **Continue** until help arrives or person shows signs of life
7. **If trained**: 30 compressions → 2 breaths (repeat)

### Burns
| Degree | Appearance | Treatment |
|--------|------------|-----------|
| 1st | Red, painful, no blisters | Cool water 10-20 min, aloe, monitor |
| 2nd | Blisters, severe pain | Cool water, loosely cover, seek care |
| 3rd | White/charred, numb | Cover with clean dry cloth, **call 911** |

**Never**: Ice, butter, ointments on severe burns, pop blisters

### Allergic Reaction / Anaphylaxis
**Signs**: Trouble breathing, swelling of face/throat, hives, rapid heartbeat, dizziness
1. **Use EpiPen** if available — inject into outer thigh, hold 3 seconds
2. **Call 911** immediately
3. **Lie down** with legs elevated
4. **Second EpiPen** if no improvement in 5-10 minutes
5. **Monitor** for rebound symptoms

### Stroke (FAST)
| Letter | Check |
|--------|-------|
| **F**ace | Ask to smile — is one side drooping? |
| **A**rms | Ask to raise both arms — does one drift down? |
| **S**peech | Ask to repeat a phrase — is it slurred/strange? |
| **T**ime | **Call 911 immediately** — note when symptoms started |

### Heart Attack
**Signs**: Chest pressure/pain, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, pain radiating to arm/jaw/back
1. **Call 911** immediately
2. **Aspirin** — chew 325mg if not allergic and not given by EMS
3. **Rest** — sit or semi-recline, stay calm
4. **Loosen** tight clothing
5. **CPR** if person becomes unconscious and not breathing

### Seizure
1. **Clear** the area of dangerous objects
2. **Time** the seizure
3. **Do NOT** restrain, put anything in mouth, or give water/food
4. **Roll** person onto side after seizure ends (recovery position)
5. **Call 911** if: first seizure, >5 minutes, repeated seizures, injury, pregnancy, diabetes

### Poisoning
1. **Call Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222** (US)
2. **Do NOT** induce vomiting unless instructed
3. **Identify** the substance if possible
4. **Monitor** breathing and consciousness

- Thermometer
