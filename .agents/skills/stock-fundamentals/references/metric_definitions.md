# Metric Definitions and Edge Cases

## 1. PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings-to-Growth)
*   **Formula:** Forward P/E Ratio / Expected EPS Growth Rate (usually 5-year expected)
*   **Rationale:** Normalizes P/E by factoring in expected growth. A PEG < 1.0 suggests the stock is undervalued relative to its earnings growth.
*   **Edge Cases:** Meaningless for companies with negative earnings or negative expected growth.

## 2. P/E vs 5-Year Median
*   **Formula:** Current P/E / 5-Year Median P/E
*   **Rationale:** Compares current valuation against the company's historical norm, smoothing out market-wide P/E expansion/contraction.
*   **Pitfalls:** Can be misleading if the company's fundamental business model has changed dramatically in the last 5 years.

## 3. YoY EPS Growth (Earnings Per Share)
*   **Formula:** (Current EPS - Prior Year EPS) / Abs(Prior Year EPS)
*   **Rationale:** Bottom-line growth is the ultimate driver of stock price over the long term. Target > 10%.
*   **Pitfalls:** One-time tax benefits, asset sales, or massive share buybacks can artificially inflate EPS. Look at operating income to verify.

## 4. YoY Revenue Growth
*   **Formula:** (Current Revenue - Prior Year Revenue) / Prior Year Revenue
*   **Rationale:** Top-line growth. Cost-cutting can only boost EPS for so long; eventually, a company must sell more goods/services. Target > 8%.
*   **Edge Cases:** Pre-revenue biotech/tech companies will fail this and require entirely different valuation models (e.g., TAM penetration, DCF on future cash flows).

## 5. Debt-to-Equity (D/E) Ratio
*   **Formula:** Total Liabilities / Total Shareholders' Equity
*   **Rationale:** Measures financial leverage and solvency risk.
*   **Sector Adjustments:**
    *   **Technology:** Usually capital-light, so D/E should ideally be < 1.0.
    *   **Utilities / Telecom:** Capital-intensive, predictable cash flows. D/E up to 2.5 is normal.
    *   **Financials (Banks):** Inherently highly leveraged. D/E is often less useful; focus on Tier 1 Capital Ratio or Return on Tangible Common Equity (ROTCE) instead.

## 6. Free Cash Flow (FCF) Yield
*   **Formula:** Free Cash Flow per Share / Current Share Price
*   **Rationale:** FCF is Operating Cash Flow minus Capital Expenditures. Yield shows how much cash the company generates relative to its market value. Target > 5%.
*   **Edge Cases:** REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) use Funds From Operations (FFO) or Adjusted FFO (AFFO) instead of FCF, due to massive non-cash depreciation charges.

## 7. Dividend Payout Ratio
*   **Formula:** Annual Dividends per Share / Annual EPS
*   **Rationale:** Shows what percentage of earnings is paid to shareholders. 30%-60% is a healthy range, leaving room for dividend growth and reinvestment.
*   **Pitfalls:** A payout ratio > 100% means the company is paying out more than it earns, which is unsustainable and often precedes a dividend cut. Exception: REITs are legally required to pay out 90% of taxable income.
