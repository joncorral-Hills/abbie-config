# Indicator Guide for Technical Analysis

## Concept and Interpretation

### Moving Averages (SMA / EMA)
- **Concept:** Smooths price data to identify trend direction. EMA gives more weight to recent prices.
- **Interpretation:** Price above MA = uptrend. MA slopes up = strong trend.
- **False Signals:** Whipsaws in sideways/ranging markets.

### Relative Strength Index (RSI)
- **Concept:** Momentum oscillator measuring speed and change of price movements (0-100).
- **Interpretation:** > 70 generally means overbought; < 30 means oversold.
- **Filtering:** In a strong uptrend, RSI can stay overbought for long periods. Do not short solely based on overbought RSI. Look for divergences (price makes higher high, RSI makes lower high) as stronger reversal signals.

### MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)
- **Concept:** Trend-following momentum indicator showing relationship between two EMAs (usually 12 and 26).
- **Interpretation:** MACD crossing above Signal line = bullish. Crossing below = bearish. Best used when crossing from extremes (far above or below zero line).
- **Filtering:** Lags price action. Confirmation tool rather than a leading indicator.

### Bollinger Bands
- **Concept:** Volatility bands placed above and below a moving average (standard deviations).
- **Interpretation:** Price touching bands may indicate over-extension. "Squeeze" (bands narrow) precedes high volatility breakouts.
- **Filtering:** A breakout from a squeeze must be confirmed with high volume.

## Multi-Timeframe Confirmation
Always analyze higher timeframes to determine the dominant trend before taking trades on lower timeframes.

## Signal Confluence
The strongest setups occur when multiple indicators point in the same direction.

## When NOT to Trust Technicals
- **Earnings Gaps:** Fundamental news overrides technical patterns.
- **Low-Float / Penny Stocks:** Prone to extreme manipulation; technicals often fail.
- **Illiquid Markets:** Wide bid-ask spreads make standard indicators noisy and unreliable.
